boot.watch, the other end of the line

OmaBoot?'s own small server. The network tests talk to it, so a working connection is proven byte by byte, not assumed.

Why it's needed

Getting an address doesn't prove a network works: a sign-in page, a proxy or a flaky adapter can all look connected. Data that comes back exactly as it should can't be faked.

What it keeps: nothing

No request log, no addresses on disk, nothing about your Mac in the data. Rate limits live in memory and are forgotten after an hour; it counts only totals. Its uptime is public at status.boot.watch.

Next: your phone planned

Pair a phone by QR code and boot.watch relays its part in the camera, microphone and sound tests, encrypted end to end. The relay stores nothing either.

Your exact Mac, not just the model

  • One identifier, several Macs. MacBook Pro (15-inch, Early 2011) [MacBookPro8,2] shipped with three different graphics chips, and Linux drives each one differently.
  • It reads what's inside. The board ID and the chips' IDs pick the one matching configuration of 206; when two could fit, you choose.
  • Your results count there. On {{DIO}}, for that exact configuration, so the next person with the same Mac knows what to expect.

Your disks stay untouched

Before anything else, every internal disk is set read-only. Nothing is installed and no firmware or startup setting changes. Pull the stick and your Mac is as it was.
