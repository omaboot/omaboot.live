The first time you save a report to the stick, OmaBoot? Live adds a small partition for reports in the space left over.

macOS

Easiest: balenaEtcher. Flash from file, the stick, Flash.

Or in Terminal

  1. Find the stick's disk number:

    diskutil list external

  2. Flash it, with your number for N:

    diskutil unmountDisk /dev/diskN
sudo dd if={{ISO}} of=/dev/rdiskN bs=4m
diskutil eject /dev/diskN

If macOS says it can't read the disk, choose Ignore.

Linux

Easiest: GNOME Disks. Pick the stick, open the menu, Restore Disk Image…, the .iso, start.

Or in a terminal

  1. Find the stick (TRAN says usb):

    lsblk -o NAME,SIZE,MODEL,TRAN

  2. Flash the whole disk, not a partition:

    sudo dd if={{ISO}} of=/dev/sdX bs=4M status=progress oflag=sync

Windows

Easiest: balenaEtcher, as on macOS.

Or

Rufus: pick the stick and the .iso, then Start. When it asks how to write the image, choose Write in DD Image mode.

dd overwrites whatever disk you give it, without asking. Check the size and name twice.

Boot it

  1. Plug in. The stick straight into the Mac, not a hub. On a laptop, the charger too.
  2. Hold Option (Alt on a non-Apple keyboard) as the Mac starts, until the startup disks appear.
  3. Pick OmaBoot? Live and press Return. A short menu shows for 5 seconds; its first entry starts the tests.

The choice is for this start only. Next time, the Mac starts from its own disk as usual. When you're done, shut down and pull the stick.

  • No stick listed? Try another USB port or another stick. Some older Macs don't list every stick.
  • Firmware password? The Mac asks for it first.
  • T2 Mac (2018–2020)? It starts from a stick only once you allow it: T2 Macs, step by step.
